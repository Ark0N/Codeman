**Mac app: full screen over the whole display.** On a MacBook with a camera housing (the notch), macOS's full screen leaves a black bar across the top of the display. The Mac app's full screen now covers the whole display instead: Codeman's header moves up beside the camera, the tabs go around it on both sides, the toolbar stays at the right, and the terminal gains the height of the bar. The green button, the menu item, ⌃⌘F and the launch into full screen all take it, and the menu bar and the Dock still slide in when the pointer is pushed against their edge. It is on by default and can be turned off with **View > Full Screen Uses the Area Beside the Camera** or the same switch in **App Settings > Appearance > Mac App** (both only show on a Mac whose display has a camera housing); switching it while the window is in full screen changes it over at once. Off gives macOS's own full screen back, which is a Space of its own: the whole-display kind covers the desktop it is on instead. The page stays below the camera, as before, with a Codeman server from before this release and while the tabs are grouped by state, by case or as a ledger in the header strip.
